# BHOOMI

**Demand-Driven Agriculture, Contract Farming & B2B Procurement Platform**

> Connecting Farmers with Real Market Demand

A practical concept note outlining the idea, the problems it solves, how the model is expected to work, and the path from idea to pilot.

**Status:** Idea generation & concept development stage. No commercial operations have started yet.

---

## What BHOOMI is about

BHOOMI is a proposed AgriTech startup built on a simple shift in thinking: stop growing first and then scrambling for buyers. Start with what the market actually needs, organise farmers to grow exactly that, and manage the whole chain from field to buyer.

In India, a huge number of small and marginal farmers work with tiny plots or leased land. They put money into seeds, fertiliser, labour and lease rent, yet still struggle to find buyers who will pay a fair price. At the same time, companies in the herbal, medicinal, food-processing and nutraceutical space need specific crops in bulk, with consistent quality and reliable delivery. Finding farmers who can meet those requirements is often hard.

BHOOMI sits right in that gap. The working principle is simple:

```
DEMAND → PRODUCTION → PROCUREMENT
```

First understand what buyers want. Then match them with the right farmers and manage everything from the field all the way to the buyer.

---

## The problems we are trying to address

Most of the challenges centre on small and marginal farmers.

### Limited land and high input costs
Many farmers either own very small plots or take land on lease. Lease rent, seeds, fertiliser and labour all have to be paid upfront. When the crop doesn’t fetch a decent price, recovering that investment becomes difficult.

### No direct link to large buyers
Farmers growing specialised crops rarely know which companies need their produce, what quantity is required, or what quality standards the buyer expects. That connection simply doesn’t exist in most cases.

### Uncertain demand
A lot of cultivation still happens on the basis of past experience or local market trends. After harvest, farmers start looking for buyers. By then, prices may have dropped or the quality may not match what the buyer wants.

### Weak production planning
Without clear information on industrial demand, farmers find it hard to plan variety, volume and harvest timing in a way that actually matches buyer needs.

### Financial risk
When the crop doesn’t sell at a reasonable price, farmers struggle to cover cultivation costs and lease payments. That risk is exactly what BHOOMI wants to reduce by tying production to confirmed demand wherever possible.

## The proposed solution

BHOOMI is designed as a demand-driven procurement platform, not a typical marketplace where produce is listed after harvest. The approach is **buyer-first**.

In practice the platform would:

- Identify companies that need specific crops in volume
- Collect their quantity, quality, delivery and price expectations
- Find suitable farmers or Farmer Producer Organisations (FPOs)
- Help those farmers plan cultivation according to the buyer’s requirements
- Support with seeds and basic guidance where it makes commercial sense
- Monitor the crop and organise collection after harvest
- Handle cleaning, grading and basic processing as needed
- Supply the aggregated produce to the B2B buyer
- Also connect farmers with wholesale vegetable vendors for regular fresh produce supply

The aim is a more organised link between what is grown and what the market actually needs.

---

## How the model is expected to work

### Step 1 – Capture buyer requirements
Approach Ayurvedic, herbal, food-processing and nutraceutical companies. Get clarity on crop, quantity, quality specs, delivery date and location, expected price range, and any certification or processing needs.

### Step 2 – Find the right farmers
Match the requirement with farmers or FPOs based on location, available land, experience with the crop, and capacity to deliver the required volume.

### Step 3 – Production planning
Share the buyer’s specifications with the selected farmers — variety, seed needs, cultivation practices, quality parameters, expected quantity and harvest window. Where feasible, arrange seeds or other inputs.

### Step 4 – Cultivation and monitoring
Farmers grow the crop as planned. Field agents and agricultural experts keep an eye on progress and provide guidance when needed.

### Step 5 – Collection and quality check
After harvest, produce is collected and checked against the buyer’s quality parameters.

### Step 6 – Cleaning, grading and processing
Depending on what the buyer wants, the produce may be cleaned, graded, dried, powdered, or processed further (including essential oil extraction where relevant) and packed.

### Step 7 – Bulk supply to the buyer
The aggregated, quality-checked material is delivered to the industrial or wholesale buyer. That completes the chain from field to industry.

## Initial crop focus

Starting with a small set of specialised crops that already have industrial demand makes more sense than trying everything at once.

| Crop | Focus | Notes |

## Additional vertical – Wholesale vegetable supply

Alongside specialised crops, there is a second, more everyday opportunity: connecting farmers directly with vegetable vendors and wholesale traders who operate in mandis.

Many vendors currently buy from traditional wholesale markets. BHOOMI can create an alternative channel by matching their daily or weekly requirements with farmers who can supply them, then handling collection, sorting, grading and transport.

### How this side would run

1. Vendors share their requirements (type, quantity, quality, delivery schedule, location, expected price)
2. Suitable farmers are identified
3. Produce is collected from the field
4. It is sorted, graded and aggregated as per the vendor’s needs
5. Delivery is organised to the mandi vendor or wholesale trader

For farmers this means extra market access and less dependence on a single selling channel. For vendors it means a more organised and quality-focused source of supply.

## How the business can make money

Revenue is expected to come from the procurement and supply-chain work itself, **not** from charging farmers high fees.

- **Procurement margin** — Earn a margin on the difference between the price paid to farmers and the price charged to the buyer
- **Buyer service fees** — Companies may pay for farmer identification, production arrangement, quality coordination and logistics management
- **Processing and value addition** — Cleaning, grading, drying, powdering, essential-oil extraction and packaging
- **Logistics and aggregation** — Charges for collection, storage, transport and bulk delivery
- **Vegetable supply margin** — Similar procurement margin and coordination fee on the fresh vegetable side
- **Input facilitation (later)** — Possible collaboration with seed and input companies once the network is running

All of this depends on actual commercial agreements and successful operations on the ground.

## Existing players and how BHOOMI is different

The AgriTech space already has several platforms:

| Platform | Focus |
|----------|-------|
| **DeHaat** | Inputs, advisory, finance and market linkage for farmers |
| **Ninjacart** | Connects producers with traders, wholesalers and retailers (fresh produce) |
| **Arya.ag** | Commodity trading, storage, logistics and related finance |
| **AgriBazaar** | Online marketplace with trading, auctions, quality checks, logistics, contracts |
| **e-NAM** | Government electronic trading platform linking mandis |

**BHOOMI is different.** It is not trying to be another marketplace that simply lists produce after harvest. The focus is on:

1. Identifying demand first
2. Matching farmers to that demand
3. Organising production to specification
4. Monitoring the crop
5. Collecting and verifying quality
6. Delivering the required quantity to B2B buyers

The wholesale vegetable network is an additional, parallel vertical. The goal is a specialised, execution-heavy, demand-driven procurement system.

## A simple working example

Suppose an Ayurvedic company needs **100 metric tonnes of Ashwagandha** after six months. It approaches BHOOMI with its quality and quantity requirements. BHOOMI studies the need, identifies suitable farmers or FPOs in a region such as Uttar Pradesh, shares seed and cultivation guidance, monitors the crop, collects and verifies the produce after harvest, aggregates the required volume, and supplies it to the company.

On the vegetable side, if a wholesaler needs **500 kg of certain vegetables every day**, the same logic applies — identify farmers, coordinate collection and quality, and arrange regular supply.

These are only illustrations of how the model could work. They are not confirmed orders or guaranteed outcomes.

## Starting lean — no need for a big app on day one

The model can be tested without heavy technology investment. Early tools can be as simple as:

- WhatsApp for day-to-day communication
- Excel or Google Sheets for farmer and buyer records
- Field agents for on-ground coordination
- A basic website or form for collecting buyer requirements
- Local partners for processing and transport

**First validation target:**

```
1 buyer  →  10 farmers  →  1 crop  →  1 complete procurement cycle
```

The point is to prove that the commercial model works before spending money on apps and infrastructure.

---

## Protecting farmers from unnecessary risk

Farmers should not be pushed into cultivation on the basis of vague or uncertain demand. Practical safeguards:

- Buyer requirements should be verified before any cultivation starts
- Written agreements should clearly state price, quality standards and payment timelines
- Farmers need a realistic picture of the costs they will incur
- Quality testing and rejection rules must be transparent
- Crop failure and price risks should be discussed openly and reflected in agreements where possible

The aim is better market access for farmers, **not** a promise of guaranteed profit.

## The longer-term vision

BHOOMI is not meant to stay a college project. The hope is to turn it into a proper AgriTech company that gives farmers better access to real demand and supplies industries with reliable agricultural raw material.

Right now the work is about clarifying the concept, understanding market demand, talking to potential buyers and farmers, and preparing a practical pilot. Once the model is validated on a small scale, the plan is to expand carefully across crops and geographies.

> **Vision:** A demand-driven agricultural system where farmers grow what the market needs and businesses get consistent, quality-focused supply.

## Closing note

BHOOMI is built around a straightforward idea: identify what the market needs first, then organise farmers to produce it. By linking small and marginal farmers with industrial and wholesale buyers, managing production to specification, and handling procurement and quality, the platform aims to create value on both sides.

Alongside specialised crops, the wholesale vegetable network offers a second, more regular flow of business. Together, the two verticals can support a more organised agricultural supply chain.

The project is still early. The immediate focus is **validation, not scale**. If the pilot works, the same logic can be expanded carefully into more crops, more regions and stronger technology support.

```
First understand the demand.
Then organise the production.
```
